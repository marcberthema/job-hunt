## How to interpret this audit

- Age-based archives are not evidence of a skill deficit or a confirmed job closure.
- Explicit skip notes take precedence over earlier scoring concerns. In particular, EPAM Azure/AKS ended as a paywall skip, Petal as a stale listing, and Teladoc as a compensation gate.
- When only an assessment survives, the classification is labelled assessment-based. It does not claim to reconstruct an undocumented personal decision. Exo and GDMS remain unclear.
- A gap means the required depth or specialization was not demonstrated in the saved assessment. It does not necessarily mean no experience at all.
- Historical assessments sometimes overstated Azure/Terraform ownership and understated older AWS, Windows/AD and networking experience. Their scores are not fresh ratings against the corrected profile.
- Old compensation decisions used higher targets. Aequilibrium and Merge by Merative scored 9 but were flagged on pay; their historical thresholds should not automatically be reused today.
- Suspected geography or clearance restrictions are marked uncertain; E-Verify language alone was not treated as proof of ineligibility.
- Current board reports can reuse generic search URLs or refer to a different posting of the same title. Ledger decisions and recovered historical evidence take precedence.
- No live job availability checks or rescoring were performed. applications.md was not changed.

## Main technical themes

1. **Production Kubernetes ownership:** operating clusters, AKS/EKS, networking/ingress, upgrades, reliability and platform architecture. Deploying applications with Argo CD is relevant experience, but does not establish cluster ownership. Examples: Caseware, Nango, Sophos, ELITS, Orion/Mogo.
2. **Production AWS and infrastructure ownership:** multi-account design, landing zones, IAM, EKS, CloudFormation/CDK and large migrations. Earlier AWS exposure should be credited; it is different from years owning these systems in production. Examples: Apptoza, BMO, healthcare non-profit, Turing IT.
3. **Software-heavy platform/FDE work:** production Go, TypeScript/Node, React, C#, distributed application design and staff-level product architecture. Python automation is a strength but does not satisfy every application-development requirement.
4. **Specific ecosystems:** GCP, OpenShift/Kafka, Databricks, NGINX, ServiceNow integration, Redis/ElastiCache, FinOps/policy-as-code, identity/security architecture. These are job-specific gaps rather than a general lack of DevOps ability.
5. **AI roles split into distinct specialties:** D-Wave required production LLM/RAG/agent integrations; Boson and Exaion required GPU/CUDA/HPC; Syndesus required Jetson and industrial protocols. Those are different learning paths.
6. **Leadership or different career tracks:** formal people management, director budgets/headcount, staff architecture, frontend/backend development, corporate IT, mainframe, carrier networking and EDA tooling. Many skips reflect the kind of work requested rather than a skill worth pursuing.

## Decisions worth revisiting against today’s profile

- **Aequilibrium / Merge by Merative:** old compensation targets; original high technical scores also contained overstated Azure ownership, so rescore before pursuing.
- **CARET:** earlier Windows/AD/infrastructure experience was overlooked; verify recency and desired job scope.
- **Apptoza / Jobgether / GlobalLogic / Lazer:** replace “no AWS” reasoning with an honest assessment of production depth and required services.
- **Empire Life:** a missing literal Platform Engineer title is not a valid technical gap; formal management requirements can still matter.
- **Exo / GDMS:** decision evidence is insufficient; do not invent a pay, clearance or location rejection.
